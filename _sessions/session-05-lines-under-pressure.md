## Summary

Session 5 shifts the crew from discovery into preparation. Evan spends his days inside [The Penumbra]({{ '/world/locations/the-penumbra/' | relative_url }}) tracing its visible utility structure through **B1**, learning where power, water, gas, phone, and camera systems actually enter the building and where they break out floor by floor, while also discovering one of the session's most unsettling absences: nothing in the ordinary infrastructure seems to run down into **B2** at all. Once he shares that map with Heylorn, the hacker turns it into leverage by building small radio-controlled shutoff devices that can be hidden in breaker boxes and triggered later if the job needs confusion. At the same time, Heylorn and Robert follow the thread offered by the kitchen staff and spend hours drinking with the Penumbra's chefs, only to find their unease sharpened rather than relieved by how normal, warm, and convincingly decent the men appear. Meanwhile, Caleb turns outward, leaning on old survivalist-world contacts and repeated calls with Mr Nobody to assemble what the job now requires: funding, tools, weapons, armor, hired labor for a street-level distraction, and a few reliable heavies to keep an exit open once the crew makes its run at Vault 9. By the end of the session, the crew has done more than gather clues. They have started building the machine that might carry them into the lower Penumbra.

## Key Scenes

### Penumbra's Arteries

<details markdown="1">
<summary markdown="span">Evan maps the Penumbra's utility structure, and Heylorn turns the building's breaker boxes into possible sabotage points.</summary>
<div class="details-prose" markdown="1">
Evan kept his head down and his hands busy, which made him useful and therefore nearly invisible. That was exactly what he needed.

Over the course of several workdays inside [The Penumbra]({{ '/world/locations/the-penumbra/' | relative_url }}), he started paying closer attention to the building's practical anatomy rather than only its rhythms. In **B1**, he found the utility room where the main trunk lines entered the structure: power, telephone, gas, and water, all feeding in from the city before branching back out toward the upper floors. The room also held the relevant electrical panels, breakers, and the branch circuit for the casino's CCTV coverage. Evan did not fully understand the technical meaning of what he was seeing, but he understood enough to catalog it carefully. Panel locations. Breaker groupings. Which feeds appeared to support which parts of the building. Which boxes might be opened without immediately drawing attention.

He kept going.

At another point in the week, Evan made his way up to the roof and identified the Penumbra's air-conditioning units and exhaust stacks, adding one more layer to the crew's map of how the building breathed. The roof told him something the basement had already begun to suggest: the visible systems of the Penumbra behaved like those of a normal building right up until they did not.

The real break came from what he could not find. For all the obvious utility logic feeding **B1** and the public floors above, Evan found no corresponding lines whatsoever leading down into **B2**. No conventional power run. No visible water feed. No normal extensions of the systems he had spent days tracing. The second basement appeared, at least from the standpoint of ordinary infrastructure, to be disconnected from the rest of the building.

When Evan brought his notes back to Suite 1108, Heylorn immediately saw the opportunity hidden inside details Evan had mostly collected by instinct. Over the next stretch of downtime, he built a set of short-range radio-controlled devices small enough to conceal inside selected breaker boxes. If Evan could place them without discovery, Heylorn would gain the ability to remotely kill chosen sections of the Penumbra's utilities at the right moment: a pocket blackout here, a communications interruption there, a sudden systems fault timed to pull attention away from where the crew actually intended to move.

The Penumbra was becoming less like a mystery and more like a machine. Machines could be interrupted.

### Happy Hour

<details markdown="1">
<summary markdown="span">Heylorn and Robert spend hours with the Penumbra's chefs and come away more unsettled by their normalcy than by any visible slip.</summary>
<div class="details-prose" markdown="1">
The invitation from the chef was too useful to ignore and too dangerous to trust. That alone made it worth taking.

Heylorn and Robert showed up to the Friday-night pub gathering carrying the same tension they had brought to every Penumbra-adjacent conversation since Caleb's report from the kitchen. In their minds, the working theory was already monstrous: whatever happened inside the casino's culinary machinery was connected to the vanished laborers and the horror Caleb believed he had seen. That meant the chefs should have been butchers in the worst sense of the word. Complicit. Predatory. Just waiting for the wrong remark to reveal what they really were.

Instead, they got happy hour.

The chefs drank, laughed, traded complaints about impossible customers and long shifts, argued about knife care, swapped old kitchen-disaster stories, and behaved like exactly what they appeared to be: overworked professionals blowing off steam after service. They were coarse in the ordinary way, generous in the ordinary way, exhausted in the ordinary way. If they were performing, then the performance never cracked once across several hours. No odd euphemism slipped loose. No shared glance lingered too long. No one said anything that sounded like a confession disguised as a joke.

That normalcy was not comforting. It was worse.

By the time the gathering wound down, Heylorn and Robert were left with three possibilities, none of them good. Either the chefs were monsters with immaculate masks. Or they were decent men participating in something horrific without knowing the full shape of it. Or worst of all, the kitchen contained some mixture of the two and there was no clean way to tell the difference from the outside.

They left with no new names, no obvious leverage, and no proof that Caleb had been wrong. But they did leave with a sharper understanding of the real problem: the Penumbra did not merely hide secrets behind hostile faces. It hid them behind ordinary ones.

### General Contractor

<details markdown="1">
<summary markdown="span">Caleb turns family ties, survivalist contacts, and calls with Mr Nobody into the beginnings of a real heist operation.</summary>
<div class="details-prose" markdown="1">
Caleb spent the week doing what he did best when obsession and logistics found common cause: making too many calls, pushing too hard, and somehow getting results anyway.

He reached back toward the world he came from, reopening messy lines of contact with old colleagues, relatives, and fellow true believers still orbiting the survivalist compound that had shaped him. Some of those conversations were arguments. Some were negotiations. Some were just old emotional wreckage put to practical use. Alongside them ran repeated calls to Mr Nobody, who remained exactly as he had always been: interested, encouraging, and infuriatingly willing to help just enough.

Caleb's goals were not subtle, but they were coherent. First, he wanted a loud and ugly construction project staged on the streets outside [The Penumbra]({{ '/world/locations/the-penumbra/' | relative_url }}), something legitimate-looking enough to exist in public and disruptive enough to pull eyes, noise, and civic attention toward the front of the building when the crew needed it. Second, he wanted the opposite kind of labor underground: a quieter effort to dismantle the blocked end of the abandoned subway spur and create a direct access point into **B3**. One project would advertise itself. The other would need to happen almost invisibly.

He also began assembling the material side of the job. Firearms. Cameras. Lockpicks. Specialized tools. Protective gear, including bulletproof vests. Not because the crew wanted a firefight, but because wanting had very little to do with whether one occurred. And beyond equipment came people. Caleb worked to secure a few hired heavies willing to accompany the crew part of the way, then hold the line on their exit while the real vault team pushed inward toward Vault 9.

By the end of the week, Caleb had not solved every logistical problem. But he had done something just as important: he had transformed the heist from an idea into a procurement chain. The crew was no longer talking about what they might need someday. They were arranging for concrete, steel, manpower, weapons, and timing.

The job was beginning to cast a shadow outside the walls of the Penumbra itself.
