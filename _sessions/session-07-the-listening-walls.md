## Summary

Session 7 carries the crew out of the last recognizable architecture below [The Penumbra]({{ '/world/locations/the-penumbra/' | relative_url }}) and into a lower world that no longer behaves like a guarded basement. After descending from **B3** into a bedrock chamber vast enough to swallow their flashlight beams, they force open a colossal stone door and enter the processional halls of **B4**, where pale lanterns burn without warmth and the very walls seem able to turn private doubt into spoken pressure. Evan, Robert, Caleb, and Heylorn each nearly lose themselves to that assault before Caleb snaps the group back into motion and keeps the descent from ending in panic. Beyond the halls, the crew slips through a great causeway patrolled by towering pale custodians, reaches the route toward **Vault 9**, and discovers that the final approach is not a vault door at all but a vertical drop into darkness. At the bottom, they find not steel, treasure racks, or a monster's lair, but a domed cavern filled with still water, a green island, and a one-room cottage lit by firelight. When the cabin door finally opens, the figure waiting inside welcomes them in and introduces itself simply as **D**.

## Key Scenes

### The Door Under B3

<details markdown="1">
<summary markdown="span">The crew reaches B4, crosses a chamber of open bedrock, and forces their way through an enormous stone door into the lower vaultworks.</summary>
<div class="details-prose" markdown="1">
The spiral stair from **B3** did not end in another service room or another ring of practical defenses. It ended on a landing cut into black bedrock, with darkness spreading so far in every direction that the crew's lights could not find the chamber walls.

That alone was enough to stop them for a moment.

Ahead stood what first looked like a distant shape in the stone and slowly resolved, as they crossed the chamber, into a door far larger than they had understood from the landing. It rose roughly twelve feet high beneath an arched top, carved from the same smooth stone as everything around it. Only at close range could they even tell it was made from two halves. The fit between those halves was so exact that the seam looked less like a join than a flaw in perception.

The crew hesitated there too. They had already passed beneath the Penumbra, beneath its hidden sublevels, beneath what should have been the deepest practical infrastructure in the building. This place did not feel like more of the same. It felt like they had reached the edge of a different category of space.

But turning back would have meant admitting that the job ended at the threshold.

So they put hands to stone and pushed together. The doors gave slowly, enormously, with the stubborn weight of something that had not been opened casually in a very long time. A gust of stale air rolled out through the widening gap. Beyond it waited tall halls, pale lantern light, and the unmistakable sense that the descent was no longer happening inside any normal definition of a basement.
</div>
</details>

### The Listening Walls

<details markdown="1">
<summary markdown="span">As the crew advances through B4's processional halls, the space itself begins pressing on their minds until Caleb forces the group back under control.</summary>
<div class="details-prose" markdown="1">
The processional halls stretched ahead in long, severe lines: broad corridors with arched ceilings, perfectly carved alcoves, and pale metal lanterns throwing cool white light instead of torchfire. The arches overhead resembled mine supports at first glance, but there was no sense that these had been added to stabilize anything. They had been carved directly from the same bedrock as the hall itself, as though the place had grown into that shape rather than been constructed.

The crew moved carefully, splitting their line across both sides of the corridor and advancing at the kind of pace men use when they are trying not to admit they are afraid.

That was when the halls began talking back.

Evan heard the simplest and cruelest version of his own fear: that this was pointless, that he did not belong here, that he should leave before he made things worse. Robert spotted a manila envelope sitting in one lantern alcove, only to have it vanish when he reached for it, leaving behind the old conviction that every case ended the same way for him: too late, too incomplete, one more excuse to drink instead of try again. Caleb felt the old buzzing rise in his ears and spent precious minutes trying to decode a pattern that was never there, holding his breath so long in his fixation that he nearly forgot to breathe at all. Heylorn, looking at the others as they frayed, saw only proof that he had joined the wrong crew for the wrong kind of job and ought to retreat while retreat was still possible.

For a few dangerous moments, **B4** nearly defeated them without showing a single visible guard.

Caleb broke first, but in the useful direction. He snapped out of his stupor gasping, recognized what the space was doing to them, and forced the crew to recognize it too. Evan and Robert were already on the edge of argument, Heylorn already halfway to deciding the descent should end here, and Caleb cut through all of it with the one idea strong enough to override their panic: whatever intelligence or design governed this place clearly wanted them to lose momentum, fracture, and turn back. That realization gave the others something solid to push against. They steadied themselves, closed ranks, and kept moving.
</div>
</details>

### The Causeway Patrol

<details markdown="1">
<summary markdown="span">The crew enters a vast hall patrolled by pale giants and slips through B4 by shadowing one guard's route from a careful distance.</summary>
<div class="details-prose" markdown="1">
The halls eventually opened onto the causeway, and with that opening the scale of the lower vaultworks changed again.

Here the path ran beside a chasm through a grand hall broad enough to make the men feel reduced inside it. Parallel walkways traced the edges of the void. Down that route moved the first true custodians the crew had seen on **B4**: great gaunt beings with pale skin, long strides, and a disciplined patrol pattern that carried them through the hall in a steady clockwise rotation. Their footsteps echoed with such regular force that the sound alone became a kind of warning bell.

The crew waited only long enough to understand the rhythm before committing to it.

Rather than challenge the patrol, they slipped out behind one of the giants and kept to its wake, maintaining just enough distance not to be obvious while trusting that the guard's own motion would buy them cover. It was the kind of plan that worked only as long as everyone held their nerve. More than once they had to flatten themselves into branching corridors when separate patrols crossed paths ahead. For brief stretches they were forced into the side halls leading toward **Vault 7** and **Vault 8**, holding still in borrowed shadow until the route opened again. Each time they emerged, found their moving shelter, and resumed the slow dangerous tail.

The maneuver was not glamorous. It was tense, patient, and one mistake away from catastrophe. But it worked. By treating the giants less like opponents to defeat than like part of the architecture to move around, the crew crossed one of the most exposed stretches of the descent without ever having to test what those beings would do if directly challenged.
</div>
</details>

### The Well Of Vault 9

<details markdown="1">
<summary markdown="span">Following the route toward Vault 9, the crew finds a narrowing cavern, a circular well, and the realization that the final descent is straight down into darkness.</summary>
<div class="details-prose" markdown="1">
Beyond the causeway, the route toward **Vault 9** diverged from the larger ceremonial geometry of **B4**. The hall narrowed. The floor sloped downward. Cleanly cut stone gradually gave way to a more natural cavern shape, as though the lower level had stopped imposing order on the earth and started yielding to it.

That path ended in a rounded chamber with a circular pit in the floor.

Evan leaned over first and saw only the faintest suggestion of light somewhere far below, wavering and uncertain. Caleb answered the question of depth in the most direct way available: he pulled one of the lanterns from the wall and dropped it. For twenty feet or so, the falling light showed a cylindrical shaft of stone. Then the lantern bounced, vanished from sight, and continued falling through darkness until the crew finally heard it strike water with a distant splash.

Eighty feet, give or take. Maybe a little more.

That was enough to plan around. The crew anchored a rope near the mouth of the shaft and made peace with the fact that no staircase was coming to save them from the next step. Evan went first and paid for it, losing control partway down and dropping the rest of the distance into the freezing water below. Caleb followed with more confidence, using the rope only long enough to guide himself before letting go and cutting cleanly into the pool. Heylorn and Robert came after, steady and deliberate, climbing as far as they needed to before dropping the last stretch into the dark.

For all four of them, the descent carried the same message. Whatever **Vault 9** was, it did not expect to be reached by people who wanted comfort, order, or a reliable way back up.
</div>
</details>

### The Island In Vault 9

<details markdown="1">
<summary markdown="span">At the bottom of the well, the crew finds an impossible underground sanctuary and a cottage occupied by a shifting host who calls itself D.</summary>
<div class="details-prose" markdown="1">
What waited below did not resemble any vault the crew had imagined.

They surfaced in a broad domed cavern whose walls shimmered with veins of ore and other bright mineral traces, all of it climbing upward until the ceiling looked less like stone than a clear night sky scattered with stars. The water around them was shallow, still, and fresh. At the center of the cavern rested a small island so green and self-contained it seemed placed there from another world: grass, moss, flowers, bushes, and trees gathered around a one-room cabin lit from within by firelight. A quiet plume of smoke rose from the chimney into the glittering dark overhead.

By then the crew had descended too far to trust beauty, but the sight of it unsettled them for the opposite reason from everything above. **Vault 9** was supposed to be a target, a chamber, a final locked problem. Instead it looked almost pastoral. A refuge. A joke. A trap designed by someone who understood exactly how much more frightening kindness could be than obvious menace.

They crossed the water, approached the island, and knocked.

After a brief pause, the door opened. The person standing there resisted any stable description. In the firelight and in the space between blinks, age, race, and gender all seemed to shift. The figure regarded them with easy hospitality rather than alarm, stepped aside with a graceful sweep of one arm, and spoke in the tone of someone greeting guests who were terribly overdue.

"It has been so long since I've received a visitor," it said. "Please come in. You may call me D."

And just like that, after all the weight of the descent, the crew found the heart of **Vault 9** waiting in the shape of a warm room and an invitation.
</div>
