**Role:** High-Stakes Gambler / Paranormal Predator

## Description
"Silk" Rainer is one of the most unnerving regulars on the second floor of [The Penumbra]({{ '/world/locations/the-penumbra/' | relative_url }}). He is flawlessly calm, exquisitely dressed, and willing to bet on almost anything: money, property, favors, years of service, or human lives. What the ordinary mind perceives as a composed man flanked by two hulking bodyguards is, in truth, a compromise with the impossible.

Silk is actually a massive, sentient spider wearing itself into human perception like a well-cut lie. The two nameless bodyguards are not separate beings at all, but the non-paranormal mind's attempt to explain Silk's impossible size, extra limbs, and predatory geometry as something familiar and humanoid. Those near him for too long often leave with gaps in memory, a headache, or the certainty that something about the room was wrong.

## Appearance
- An impossibly fine gray suit, matched with a gray shirt and tie
- A composed expression and perfectly measured speech, no matter what is being risked
- Two massive, wordless "bodyguards" that behave less like staff and more like articulated extensions of his presence

## Known Information
- **Location:** [The Penumbra]({{ '/world/locations/the-penumbra/' | relative_url }}), private second-floor gaming room and adjacent VIP spaces
- **Connections:** Penumbra dealers, select house management, wealthy degenerates, occult-adjacent patrons who suspect what he is without fully understanding it
- **Goals:** Find ever more interesting wagers, feed his alien appetite for uncertainty, and indulge the thrill of risking what others find unthinkable
- **Threat Level:** Extremely dangerous if confronted outside the social rules of the table; deceptively contained while the game remains interesting

## Relationships
Silk took an immediate interest in Heylorn during the Session 3 reconnaissance, not because the stakes were impressive, but because Heylorn was willing to sit at the table at all. Silk enjoys gamblers who know they are outmatched and play anyway.

To Silk, even bets involving the lives of his offspring are not monstrous but ordinary arithmetic filtered through an alien scale of value. When one has a brood in the hundreds, the life of one child can become merely another chip in the pile.
